# Assumed parameters

Parameters of the service-recovery subsystem that are not observable in public data.
Each is stated as an explicit assumption and examined through sensitivity analysis in
the study. Ranges below are the documented assumption bands, not measurements.

| Parameter | Meaning | Assumption band |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint sensitivity | Complaints filed per service failure | 0.05 – 0.40 |
| Support-capacity unit cost | Fully loaded monthly cost per complaint-handling agent | $3,500 – $7,000 |
| Revenue-allocation rule | Share of platform net revenue directed to service-recovery investment | 1% – 10% |
| Quality-perception delay | Time for riders to update perceived service quality after experience changes | 1 – 6 months |
| Capacity-development time | Approval, funding, hiring, training, and system implementation before new capacity is installed | 3 – 12 months |
| Attrition sensitivity | Increase in rider attrition rate per unit decline in perceived quality | examined across 0.1 – 1.0 |

Notes:

- The bands are deliberately wide: the study's conclusions rest on the policy comparison
  (fixed-capacity vs. backlog-responsive vs. reserve-capacity investment) holding across the
  bands, not on any point estimate.
- The backlog-responsive policy triggers additional investment only after unresolved
  complaints reveal a sustained capacity shortage; the reserve-capacity policy maintains
  installed capacity above current workload. Both are evaluated under each funding
  condition over a five-year horizon with quarterly capacity-planning decisions.
